# References — what the bar looks like

Calibration, not a reading list. "Memorable" is a word; these are measurements.
When a candidate is on the shortlist, ask the only question this file exists for:

> **Is my idea in this company?**

That is much harder to fool yourself on than "is this memorable?"

Nothing here is to be copied, and none of it is the right *size* — several are
lifetimes of work. What transfers is the shape of the decision behind each one.

Owner: add anything you personally find brilliant, with one line on why. Your
examples are worth more than inherited ones, because the taste being calibrated is
yours.

---

### Corridor's green-screen keying work
Owner's reference. A working VFX crew, unhappy with what existed, built keying
tooling good enough to matter to an industry that had settled. **The lesson: the
strongest projects come from someone who feels the pain daily and refuses the
available answer.** Not market research — irritation plus capability.

### Bret Victor's explorable explanations
Essays where the reader manipulates the system being described and watches it
respond. **The lesson: a new interaction can be the contribution.** No new
algorithm, no new data — a new relationship between reader and idea, and the field
has been chasing it ever since.

### ffmpeg
Handles essentially every media format there has ever been, and sits underneath a
huge fraction of video on the internet. **The lesson: unglamorous and foundational
beats fashionable.** Nobody demos ffmpeg. Everything demos on top of it.

### jq
A small language for one job — reshaping JSON — that became the way a generation of
people do it. **The lesson: a tiny, sharp, composable primitive out-lives large
frameworks.** Small is a strategy, not a compromise.

### SQLite
The most deployed database in the world, and it is a library with no server, plus a
test suite far larger than the code. **The lesson: obsessive correctness is itself a
feature, and the boring decision to be embeddable was the whole game.**

### git
A content-addressed object store with a bad interface that won anyway, because the
underlying model was right. **The lesson: get the data model right and the world
forgives a lot.** Also: it was written in weeks, by someone solving his own problem.

### Redis
Data structures, over a socket, in memory, done simply enough to read the source.
**The lesson: picking an unfashionable trade-off deliberately — memory over
durability — and being honest about it, beats trying to be everything.**

### Dwarf Fortress
A simulation of absurd depth with a famously hostile interface, sustained for two
decades by one pair of hands. **The lesson: depth, pursued past the point of
reasonableness, is a form of originality nobody can copy cheaply.**

### TempleOS
A complete operating system, compiler and language written alone. Include it as a
datapoint about **scope and singular vision**, not as a model to follow. The lesson
is what one person is capable of building alone, which is far more than the usual
estimate.

### The Mother of All Demos (1968)
Mouse, hypertext, video conferencing, collaborative editing — shown working, at
once, decades early. **The lesson: the demo *was* the contribution.** Showing people
what is possible is itself the work, which is why this repository requires a
ten-second demo.

---

## What these have in common

- Someone's **own** problem, felt directly, not a problem inferred from a market.
- A **deliberate, unfashionable trade-off**, stated plainly instead of hidden.
- Either a **new primitive** others build on, or a **new interaction** that changes
  what people expect.
- **Demonstrable in seconds**, even when the thing underneath took years.
- Judgement that reads as **one person's**, not a committee's average.

## What none of them are

- A thin variation on an existing category.
- Something whose main virtue is that it was completed.
- Something that needs a paragraph of explanation before it gets interesting.
- Something a stranger would confuse with three other things.
